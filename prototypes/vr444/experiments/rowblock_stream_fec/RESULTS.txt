Wire cost is a substantial negative for small captured blocks
------------------------------------------------------------

Extra UDP payload bytes over the same VXB no-recovery stream, aggregated across
the 12 actual captured pairs. All parity, including parity after publication,
is counted. Percentages are actual record bytes, not nominal 1/K estimates.

Block rows     K=4       K=8       K=16
   16         82.588%   82.588%   82.588%
   32         60.753%   59.999%   59.999%
   64         48.369%   42.013%   41.562%
  128         38.417%   30.453%   26.416%

Many small blocks have only one or a few source fragments. Group sizes larger
than a block do not amortize parity across other blocks. The experiment therefore
demonstrates exact bounded recovery but does not justify promoting this profile:
its offered-load penalty could erase a benefit from earlier block availability.
Cross-block grouping would require a distinct checked mapping/lifetime design,
and no such optimization is silently assumed in these numbers.

Largest observed retained pending payload was 245640 bytes. The conservative
1048656-byte theoretical bound includes pending source and parity payload for
two candidates, not peak RSS, transient joins, previous publications or metadata.

No speedup, probability of recovery on a physical link, congestion behavior,
integrated overlap or headset sub-15 result is established. Source/data identities,
strict RRF grammar, decoded pixels and atomic stereo completion are preserved.
