KNOWN SUPPLY / SAMPLING PHASE SENSITIVITY -- 2026-10-09

Result and scope
Unlimited retention of every ever-observed complete pair changes run05's
original-time maximum advancing under-15ms count only from 1,637 to 1,638.
Hypothetically advancing publication by up to 8ms, clipped at each eye's
receipt, raises that count to at most 1,724. Both remain below the 1,805 good
choices necessary for strict p95<15ms across all 1,900 warm deadlines.

Changing sampling phase produces larger differences, but moving it earlier
by half or one period precedes actual xrWaitFrame return and render entry at
ALL 1,900 warm deadlines. Such samples are not an executable selector under
the recorded admission schedule. A separate bound explicitly removes them.

This branch is an offline timestamp sensitivity, not an optimization or a
runtime simulation. Native behavior and all qualification gates are unchanged.
No headset operations, native build or performance measurements occurred.
Original run05 still failed and every original artifact remains untouched.

Population and transformations
The existing candidate and source validators admit exact full-run observations.
All 2,094 ever-observed complete wire pairs have stable per-eye metadata and
unambiguous source application joins. The known universe excludes unseen or
never-complete frames; it is not proof of complete decoder/publication coverage.

The observed baseline uses only each actual snapshot or retained pair. The
known-supply alternatives relax retention and may use a pair before its first
recorded observation, provided its recorded publication time allows it. Counts
of those choices and choices absent from the original snapshot are explicit.
This relaxation does not prove actual handle ownership or retention capacity.

Two independent declared transformations are considered:
  sampling shift: add the same delta to recorded selection end and submit,
                  preserving each original selection-to-submit interval;
  publication advance: independently subtract a budget from each participating
                       eye's ready timestamp, clipped at that eye's receipt.
All participating eyes include alpha when present. The publication transform
cannot move an eye before its packet receipt. It includes all publication
delay in the hypothetical budget; it is not an estimate of removable decode
service, queueing, evidence I/O or locking cost.

Warm-deadline membership and original runtime target timestamps stay fixed.
Future-target limits are removed. Source projection quality, OpenXR rescheduling,
GPU duration, feedback, wake phase and clock uncertainty are not simulated.

For every sample the result counts whether selection precedes recorded wait
return, render entry, or required after-fence completion, and whether submit is
at/after the original runtime prediction. The nested with_recorded_admission_floor
result discards candidates violating those recorded admission times. Meeting
that floor is necessary for the fixed schedule, not sufficient proof that a
new runtime/GPU submission schedule is executable.

Counting proof
Only nodes whose age is strictly below 15ms enter the graph. At most one choice
per deadline is allowed; wire IDs strictly increase and source targets do not
regress. Deleting over-threshold choices from any legal path preserves these
constraints. Therefore the maximum good-only path is also an upper bound on
good count in any path in this model. Each path has a sequence/frame certificate.

For nearest-rank p95 to be strictly below the threshold at count N, at least
ceil(95*N/100) good choices are necessary. The reported floor(100*good/95)
ceiling, capped by warm-deadline count, is necessary only. It does not prove
that bad choices can be inserted to obtain that total count, preserve actual
ownership, or attain a live cadence. No percentile of the good-only filtered
population is presented as a measured performance result.

The +5.555555ms / 4ms case reaches exactly 1,805 good choices, the necessary

future projection quality, runtime timing feasibility or a realizable 4ms
publication improvement. Earlier negative-phase results additionally require
changing runtime admission rather than just moving selection within the frame.

What this resolves
The exact snapshot retention limit alone explains only one additional good
choice at original times within the known universe. Sampling phase and supply
publication interact discretely, so independently measured stage savings must
not be added to a latency percentile. Further evidence should expose complete
per-eye publication/eviction events and stable wait/selection ordering before
an event-driven wait or phase policy is interpreted as an executable path.
