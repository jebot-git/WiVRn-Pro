Fractional future-target horizon research, 2026-10-09
==================================================

Outcome
Advancing complete selections can reduce conditional receive age while losing
unique-frame cadence in frozen observations. This is not sufficient evidence for
the sub-15 ms target, and none of the following alternatives is a measured live
result. Both age and cadence must remain visible when evaluating this idea.

The preserved run05 diagnostic has 1,900 first submissions after the 2-second
warmup, observed p95 receive-to-submit 26.927659 ms and 89.220492 Hz. Its original
failed acquisition summary is untouched. The submitted-trace repair explains
the historical failure; all 300 original manifest files still verify.

Full horizon chooses a different encoded frame in 1,603/1,900 warm observations;
1,600 of those advance the latched application-frame identity. Three different
encoded frames reuse the same application identity. This does not reveal the
simulation/input epoch. The full-horizon hypothesis has only 1,712 first unique
frames: an early advancement can be followed by a repeated choice while waiting
for the next frame. Including all warm hypothetical submissions gives an age
p95 of 18.733491 ms; neither age statistic is an acquired latency measurement.
The narrower 31/32 horizon advances 903 application identities and has 1,687 first
unique frames. Fractions at or below 3/4 admit no newer frame in this acquisition;
rare constrained fallbacks can retain an older pair instead.

What the counterfactual proves and does not prove
counterfactual.py first verifies the complete observed selection/presentation
contract and independently validates the host source recorder, including every
frame used by a hypothetical choice. Ambiguous projection ownership, missing
application joins, aborted/dropped source evidence or inconsistent input hashes
fail analysis. It joins clocks only by encoded frame identity; shifting the host
clock by a constant leaves every client-age hypothesis unchanged in tests.

Two explicit models are reported: a local decision using the actually observed
prior pair, and a recurrent decision retaining its prior hypothetical pair. They
hold histories, frame arrival/ready times and submit times fixed. Different
ownership would affect decoder slots, repeated-render behavior, feedback,
transport, workload and later histories. Those effects are not simulated. This
is an opportunity/constraint analysis, not a forecast of a live policy's timing.
The hypothetical cadence is computed from first unique encoded choices at the
original submit times, with repeats excluded. No promotion gate is evaluated.

Prototype
horizon_policy.h is standalone and opt-in through Config.enabled. Disabled
behavior reproduces legacy closest-target exactly. Fraction 1/1 reproduces the
existing bounded-newest policy. Other ratios only reduce the existing future
replacement horizon; they preserve complete stereo/alpha metadata, exact slots,
nonregression against closest and previous coherent choices, and retained
fallback ownership. A retained fallback is not relabeled or retimed if its
original target is outside a new admission horizon. Invalid ratios, runtime
inputs and overflow fail explicitly. No native configuration flag, binary,
source-overlay or runtime selector has been changed by this branch.

Next independent approach
Investigate whether advancing only when another complete pair is already held
in reserve preserves conditional cadence. A reserve is observable at the current
snapshot, but its future availability still depends on ownership and feedback.
Require the same cadence/identity guards and keep counterfactual conclusions
separate from a later untraced hardware comparison.
