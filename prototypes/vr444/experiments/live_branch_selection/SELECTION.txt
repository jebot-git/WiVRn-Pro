Transport decision
------------------
Keep TCP. The direct ordinary TCP/UDP diagnostic's high-load p95 sender RTT
was 10.399/9.904 ms TCP and 10.057/10.062 ms UDP, with 180/180 complete pairs in
each arm. This is no decisive UDP advantage. No measured pair loss does not
establish sustained reliability or an FEC requirement.

The nominal 90 Hz setting achieved roughly 86.3–86.9 Hz. The harness waits for an
ACK, sleeps to its next start, then generates the next synthetic pair before
starting its clock: both stop-and-wait and preparation can reduce cadence.
Do not attribute the gap to the wireless link. RTT includes receiver validation
and the return ACK; receiver assembly uses its own local clock. Neither equals
one-way delivery or native latency. Short synthetic bursts did not test codecs,
overlapping production pacing, batching, GSO/GRO, FEC, encryption, or OpenXR.

Retain research/sub15-20261009/wireless-pair-probe (executed source 9ec0b0d1)
as a diagnostic. If investigating UDP further, first qualify preparation-aware
pacing and overlapping complete-pair ownership, then realistic loss/FEC. The
older udp-batch 3075ff7c and udp-offload 74e678a1 evidence remains loopback only.
