This is source research and a proposed experiment sequence; no candidate was executed and no headset/network was accessed.

The user identifies **Quest Pro rooted with Magisk through Singularity, Intel NUC 12 Extreme i7, and direct 2.5 GbE Ethernet from the host to an ASUS RT-AXE7800**, with a 6 GHz network available. ## Three primary transport comparisons

| Arm | Hypothesis | Critical limit and first falsification |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Secured, paced UDP/VXF | Explicit bounded queues, expiry and selective repair preserve freshness with low overhead. Reuse actual codec bytes. | Existing CRC/FEC is not authentication. Specify an authenticated session profile and congestion response; DTLS is one candidate. Under burst loss and a capacity step, show expired/superseded pairs stop consuming queue service and memory stays bounded. |
| QUIC DATAGRAM plus reliable control | Built-in protection and congestion control can reduce custom transport machinery while preserving unreliable media delivery. | QUIC still queues behind congestion limits; expiry/priority APIs depend on the library. Verify that expired unsent datagrams can actually be discarded and urgent control/new pairs receive bounded service. DATAGRAM has no stream flow control or automatic retransmission. |
| RTP/SRTP with RTCP feedback | Established media feedback/repair mechanisms may provide a useful alternative. | Experimental RPT/RRB payload mappings, negotiation and stereo identity need implementation. Compare no repair, one useful RTX and bounded FEC at equal total budget; reject buffering that improves delivery while reducing timely complete stereo. Native WebRTC is a separate, pinned integration arm. |

Standards basis: [UDP guidance (RFC 8085)](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc8085.html), [DTLS 1.3 (RFC 9147)](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9147.html), [QUIC DATAGRAM (RFC 9221)](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9221.html), and [WebRTC RTP/SRTP requirements (RFC 8834)](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc8834.html). These establish protocol behavior, not a performance ranking on this hardware.

Retain per-frame QUIC streams and unordered partially reliable SCTP/WebRTC data channels as narrower controls. QUIC separates stream ordering but shares congestion resources; abandoned data already transmitted cannot be recalled. SCTP message interleaving, fragmentation, sender buffering and timed abandonment need library-level checks. A large stereo message must not silently block urgent state. See [QUIC transport (RFC 9000)](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9000.html) and [data channels (RFC 8831)](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc8831.html).

## Packet and recovery constraints

Hold source traces, actual codec bytes, quality, offered rate and application cap fixed for the initial protocol comparison. Fit each transport's own framing, authentication and repair overhead within its measured path limit. The current 1,196-byte VXF record cannot simply be put inside a 1,200-byte QUIC UDP payload. QUIC DATAGRAM frames cannot fragment themselves; application sharding remains necessary. GSO does not enlarge path MTU. Include MTU reductions and avoid relying on IP fragmentation; [DPLPMTUD (RFC 8899)](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc8899.html) supplies the relevant mechanism.

The 262,144-byte limit is the experimental application admission cap, not a universal UDP limit. Retain 262,143/262,144/262,145-byte boundaries and the historical 2,223,424-byte RPT pair. Changing protocol cannot by itself make an over-cap pair admissible. Any larger cap, alternative codec, quality change or fallback is an explicit arm with bounded memory and all rejected/fallback pairs accounted for.

Derived payload-only bounds at 90 Hz are **188.744 Mbit/s** at the cap and **1.601 Gbit/s** for that historical RPT size, before protocol framing, repair or radio overhead. The latter takes 17.787 ms to serialize at a hypothetical 1 Gbit/s payload service rate. These are arithmetic bounds, not measured Wi-Fi capacity or headset timing; the RPT payload was not reproduced here. The 6 GHz band designation is not a throughput receipt.

A proposed repair decision must use remaining receiver slack against a conservative observed NACK-to-repair-completion bound plus remaining decode/upload/submit work and clock uncertainty. Do not assume RTT/2 or sum stage p95s. Start with at most one targeted repair and stop after expiry/supersession; compare against FEC at the same total load. See [RTP retransmission (RFC 4588)](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc4588.html) and [WebRTC FEC (RFC 8854)](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc8854.html).

Meta's [v53 release notes](https://communityforums.atmeta.com/blog/AnnouncementsBlog/meta-quest-build-v53-release-notes/1046442) establish Quest Pro infrastructure 6 GHz support; root is not required for that baseline. The user's wired-host/AP topology is suitable as the first controlled route. The [ASUS specifications](https://www.asus.com/ca-en/networking-iot-servers/wifi-routers/asus-wifi-routers/rt-axe7800/techspec/) list 6 GHz 2x2 operation, up to 2402 Mbit/s PHY rate, 160 MHz capability, one 2.5 GbE WAN/LAN port and four 1 GbE ports (one WAN/LAN plus three LAN). AXE7800 is an aggregate marketing rate, not headset throughput. The [quick-start guide](https://dlcdnets.asus.com/pub/ASUS/wireless/RT-AXE7800/E21333_RT-AXE7800_one-page_QSG_v2_WEB.pdf?model=RT-AXE7800) identifies the 2.5G port as default WAN; record its actual role and route before treating it as the host LAN connection.

For the standard NUC12DCMi7, [Intel's technical specification](https://www.intel.com/content/dam/support/us/en/documents/intel-nuc/NUC12DCM_NUC12EDB_TechProdSpec.pdf) lists Marvell AQC113C 10GbE with 2.5GbE negotiation. It does not list the separate I225-LM 2.5GbE controller for that i7 SKU. Verify the actual SKU/NIC, cable, connected ASUS port, configured role and negotiated full-duplex rate. The user subsequently confirmed a 2.5GbE host/AP connection. The hypothetical 1GbE bottleneck therefore does not apply to the declared setup; preserve link readback in future receipts. A 2.5GbE link does not establish sufficient wireless service or bypass the application cap. No port or router setting was changed.

Record Singularity/Magisk versions, active modules and enabled CPU/GPU/fan/build-property tweaks. [Singularity's documentation](https://github.com/Lumince/singularity/blob/main/README.md) describes such controls; their state can affect timings or reported build identity. This is configuration provenance, not a recommendation to enable them.

Host SoftAP, Quest SoftAP and Wi-Fi Direct remain capability-gated alternatives. Client 6E support does not establish 6 GHz AP/P2P support, legal channel availability or simultaneous interface combinations. Check actual driver/HAL/firmware and association receipts; see [Linux iwlwifi restrictions](https://wireless.docs.kernel.org/en/latest/en/users/drivers/iwlwifi.html), [AOSP SoftAP](https://source.android.com/docs/core/connect/wifi-softap) and [Android P2P](https://developer.android.com/develop/connectivity/wifi/wifip2p). Root does not remove [Android SELinux controls](https://source.android.com/docs/security/features/selinux) or create absent firmware features.

Use wired diagnostics to localize bottlenecks, with separate transport identities. The [fork's runner](https://github.com/jebot-git/WiVRn-Pro/blob/37c4a88bea1ff053f4f921266033b1bda50c9bf7/validation/wivrn-codecs/integration/native_compare.py) uses ADB reverse/TCP. Upstream WiVRn at `dc14ced68f98d211dccb92a60107177b3278b9ae` has a [USB network request](https://github.com/WiVRn/WiVRn/blob/dc14ced68f98d211dccb92a60107177b3278b9ae/client/application.cpp), with [device/build gating](https://github.com/WiVRn/WiVRn/blob/dc14ced68f98d211dccb92a60107177b3278b9ae/client/hmd_traits.cpp). ## Linux scheduling and radio comparisons

Compare socket-rate pacing through [fq](https://man7.org/linux/man-pages/man8/tc-fq.8.html), explicit departure scheduling through [SO_TXTIME/ETF](https://man7.org/linux/man-pages/man8/tc-etf.8.html), and bounded [sendmmsg](https://man7.org/linux/man-pages/man2/sendmmsg.2.html) batches as separate arms. ETF strict scheduling and deadline mode differ; NIC launch-time offload requires its own proof and does not schedule AP airtime. Only batch already-ready packets, handle partial sends, and never wait to fill a batch.

After that screen, compare GSO groups of 8/32 and optional GRO against ordinary datagrams. [Linux UDP](https://man7.org/linux/man-pages/man7/udp.7.html) documents segmentation and GRO receive semantics. A large host buffer is not one wire packet, and one GSO send does not prove per-segment departure spacing. Bound the latest-pending pair and scheduled horizon; socket acceptance is not permission to queue many frames.

Use [kernel timestamping](https://docs.kernel.org/networking/timestamping.html) to separate scheduler enqueue, software departure and supported hardware events; drain timestamp/error queues and associate IDs. Software TX precedes actual NIC transmission; Ethernet timestamps cannot expose later AP queues or Wi-Fi retries. Record qdisc/NIC counters, readback of requested settings and unavailable telemetry. Preserve clock domains and instrumentation overhead.

Radio screens should change one factor at a time: negotiated 80/160 MHz, default versus verified video QoS mapping, default versus Android low-latency Wi-Fi lock, and only documented aggregation controls. [RFC 8325](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc8325.html) provides DSCP/user-priority guidance; marked packets do not prove AP downlink WMM treatment. Do not give all bulk video voice priority. The [Android low-latency lock](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/net/wifi/WifiManager#WIFI_MODE_FULL_LOW_LATENCY) is conditional on runtime/app state and may trade throughput/power for latency; actual local-network/HAL behavior requires a receipt. Keep vendor defaults until evidence identifies a relevant queue.

Screening may use three alternating baseline/candidate repetitions, 10-second warmup and 30-second observations on a frozen trace; these are proposed diagnostics, not a replacement for inherited qualification runs. Avoid a full Cartesian sweep before isolating useful effects.

## Integration boundary and evidence

The pinned [test encoder](https://github.com/jebot-git/WiVRn-Pro/blob/01663069b2e92f1fc7aa0af421b74fb4f6d03250/validation/wivrn-codecs/integration/source-overlay/server/encoder/video_encoder_test.cpp) prefers the reliable control path. Merely flipping that preference does not implement bounded datagram stereo. Place an isolated adapter after immutable encoded eye envelopes; preserve owned buffers, authenticated session/configuration negotiation, and explicit complete-pair delivery. Reuse the [decoder envelope checks](https://github.com/jebot-git/WiVRn-Pro/blob/01663069b2e92f1fc7aa0af421b74fb4f6d03250/validation/wivrn-codecs/integration/source-overlay/client/decoder/test_decoder.cpp) and [StereoAdmission](https://github.com/jebot-git/WiVRn-Pro/blob/01663069b2e92f1fc7aa0af421b74fb4f6d03250/validation/wivrn-codecs/integration/source-overlay/common/codec_test/stereo_admission.h). Preserve previous-image lifetime and recheck time after repair/decode; recovery must not extend the deadline.

The [existing codec UDP adapter](https://github.com/jebot-git/WiVRn-Pro/blob/f1fe2e43924de5202de6bcffc0710639714003df/prototypes/vr444/experiments/codec_udp_interop/README.txt) carries actual codec bytes through localhost sockets, followed by synthetic 1-microsecond-per-record deadline replay. It does not yet establish authenticated native transport, live arrival deadlines or target-device performance.

Expose capability negotiation, offer-pair, bounded progress, expiry, abandonment and complete-pair delivery. Bind protocol/library/source/binary hashes, security, PMTU, FEC/repair/pacing, queue bounds, temporal codec dependencies, actual per-eye gaze sample IDs, byte accounting, timestamps and all terminal dispositions in a hashed versioned sidecar. Keep the strict v1 run manifest unchanged; QUIC reliable streams do not fit its current transport enum accurately. A planned sidecar is not a completed experiment.

Reacquire custom-codec TCP on the same 6 GHz route as a network baseline; retain TCP-over-ADB as a separate reference. Compare datagrams without FEC before contiguous/interleaved FEC at equal recovery budget. [Existing gaze telemetry](https://github.com/jebot-git/WiVRn-Pro/blob/01663069b2e92f1fc7aa0af421b74fb4f6d03250/validation/wivrn-codecs/integration/GAZE-TIMING.txt) describes pose-expression time, not camera acquisition time; do not relabel it.

## Ordered experiments, without selecting an architecture

1. Collect a capability receipt identifying exact device/OS/kernel, root and security context, host kernel/NIC/driver, AP/firmware, actual frequency/channel width, Ethernet negotiated speed/duplex and AP port role, MTU and supported timestamp/offload/socket interfaces. A missing probe is unknown, not a negative capability result.
2. Establish a common application boundary using pinned actual codec bytes and coherent whole-stereo admission. Compare secured paced UDP and QUIC DATAGRAM first to limit simultaneous changes; add RTP/SRTP after the boundary is stable. This is an implementation order, not a winning-protocol claim.
3. Run explicit candidate correctness and interoperability tests when an execution environment exists. Include malformed right eye, dimensions/identity mismatch, duplicate/reordered/missing shards, corruption, cap boundaries, oversized dense pairs, stale recovery, reconnect/configuration changes, bounded queue exhaustion and previous-image lifetime.
4. Run host/localhost diagnostics separately, then matched target trials with renderer load, asymmetric eye sizes, dense rejects, isolated/burst loss and capacity steps. Record cold setup separately from steady operation. Randomize comparison order and follow inherited repeated-run policy.
5. Compare gaze/control scheduling on shared-gaze, independent-current-gaze and independent-predicted-gaze arms. Coalesce replaceable gaze/pose state; bound reliable configuration traffic. Preserve each eye's timestamps/calibration/validity/confidence, causal prediction horizon and uncertainty/fallback, a common presentation target and coherent stereo/source/warp identity. Foveal packet priority alone cannot authorize incomplete or mixed-eye submission; optional enhancement data requires a separately declared quality/reconstruction contract.

The unchanged reference is **576x640 per eye at 90 Hz offered, earliest headset packet receipt to observation after successful xrEndFrame p95 strictly below 15 ms, and at least 88 unique coherent stereo submissions/s**, with inherited quality, memory, identity and repeated-run policy. This metric excludes host and transit before first receipt, so transport work must also expose queue/transit costs and source/gaze age with valid clock mapping and uncertainty. Report application-content novelty separately from encoded-frame cadence. Count rejected, stale, omitted, fallback and late pairs; a percentile over surviving pairs cannot hide cadence loss.

No candidate tests were reproduced. Source/receipt review, modeled bounds, localhost measurements and target hardware evidence remain separate. No neural architecture is selected, no mixed-domain ranking/training is authorized, and future training must preserve scene/session/participant splits and causality.
